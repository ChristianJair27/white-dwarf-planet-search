# WDJ203728.70+145353.15 (SDSS J203728.70+145353.0, TIC 456645161) — pulsating DB white dwarf (DBV), TESS + ZTF

Found 2026-11-12 by `wdplanets/labs/zzceti.py` (backlog 28). Verdict (updated after ZTF, same day): **pulsating DB white dwarf (DBV) detected by TESS and ZTF; pulsations unpublished as far as we can find**. Original verdict: **candidate new pulsating DB white
dwarf (DBV)**, before the ADS and ZTF checks below.

## The star
- Gaia DR3 1810475401285228928, G = 15.15, parallax 11.40 ± 0.04 mas (≈ 88 pc), BP−RP = −0.29.
- Spectroscopy (Kilic et al. 2020, ApJ 898, 84, VizieR J/ApJ/898/84/param): **DB**, Teff = 23,225 ± 1,087 K,
  log g = 7.961 ± 0.011, M = 0.582 M☉, log H/He = −5.0. That places it inside the DB instability strip
  (Teff ∼ 21,000–30,000 K for He-atmosphere pulsators, Córsico et al. 2022, A&A 668, A161, arXiv:2210.05486, sect. 1;
  Vanderbosch et al. 2022 "nearly double the number of DBVs known between 20,000 and 24,000 K").
- Gaia DR3 `phot_variable_flag` = **VARIABLE** for the white dwarf (Gaia resolves the 4" neighbour).

## TESS signal (20-s cadence, SPOC PDCSAP, CROWDSAP 0.14–0.15)
Amplitudes are corrected for crowding by SPOC, so they refer to the white dwarf's own flux.

|---|---|---|---|---|

- Both main frequencies are present in all three sectors (2022 and 2024) at the same frequency within 0.1 µHz.
- The 334.85-s amplitude is present in both halves of every sector (S55: 10.8 ± 1.1 and 15.1 ± 1.0 ppt; S81:
  13.6 ± 1.1 and 14.8 ± 0.9; S82: 10.3 ± 1.1 and 13.1 ± 1.1) — a coherent signal, not a transient.
- The third peak sits at 6,513.1 µHz = 2,986.4 + 3,526.7 µHz, the **sum frequency** of the two modes, in all
  three sectors (below threshold individually, but at the same frequency each time). Combination frequencies
  are common in white-dwarf pulsators (Vanderbosch et al. 2022 identify and set aside such peaks as combinations,
  sect. 3) and arise inside one pulsating star, so they argue against a blend of two unrelated variables.
- Periods of 280–340 s fall inside the range of DBV g-modes: Vanderbosch et al. 2022 (Table 1) list periods from
  ~180 s to ~1,120 s for their new DBVs (e.g. SDSS J081453.55+300734.8: 869.2 s and 243.24 s).
- Sector 81, 1,000 shuffles: the 0.1 % false-alarm amplitude is 4.09 ppt; the 334.85-s peak (14.36 ppt) is 3.5x it.
- After subtracting both modes, nothing within ±35 µHz of 334.85 s exceeds the threshold (residual maxima 2.8–3.3 ppt,
  <A> ≈ 0.9 ppt), so the second spike drawn next to it in the Sector 81 panel is not a separate mode at this
  sensitivity (amplitude change within the sector or window side lobe).
- Figure: dashboard/img/zzceti_WDJ203728.70+145353.15.png.

## Contamination
- Gaia DR3 1810475195126798336 at 4.1": G = 14.85 (brighter than the WD), BP−RP = 0.90, Gaia Teff 5,398 K,
  parallax 1.18 mas (≈ 850 pc), proper motion unrelated to the WD → a background G/K dwarf. Such stars do not
  pulsate at 280–340 s with percent-level amplitudes; its Gaia variability flag is "NOT_AVAILABLE" (not tested).
- Two more stars of similar colour at 20" and 34" (G 15.1, 14.3).
- TESS cannot separate 4"; attribution rests on the physics (DB in the strip, combination frequency) and on the
  Gaia VARIABLE flag of the white dwarf itself.

(the 20" and 34" neighbours are excluded in all six measurements); TESS cannot tell the two members apart (S81 favours the WD).

**2. Gaia DR3 resolves the pair** (ESA archive, `vari_summary`, `vari_classifier_result`, 2026-11-12):
- White dwarf (1810475401285228928): `phot_variable_flag` VARIABLE; 37 G-band field-of-view epochs over 926 d,
  std 15.7 mmag = 4.6 x the expected error, range 58 mmag; BP and RP std 22 and 20 mmag. Variability classifier best class
  **"WD"** (variable white dwarf), score 0.84.
- G = 14.85 neighbour (1810475195126798336): NOT_AVAILABLE (not analysed for variability).
- Two sinusoids of 14 and 10 ppt (TESS, crowding-corrected) give an rms of ~12 ppt (~13 mmag); Gaia's 15.7 mmag for the WD
  alone is consistent with the TESS pulsations belonging to the white dwarf (amplitudes of g-modes grow toward the blue,
  and G is bluer than the TESS band). If the G star were the pulsator, the WD would not show this scatter.
- Consequence for novelty: **Gaia DR3 already lists this star as a variable-white-dwarf candidate** (automatic
  classification, no period). What is new here is the detection of the pulsation periods (334.85 s, 283.55 s and their
  sum frequency) that identify it as a DBV.

## Jestin et al. 2026 table (A&A 712, A243; VizieR J/A+A/712/A243/tablea1, Harvard mirror, 864 rows)
- **The star is listed** (recno 140, Gaia DR3 1810475401285228928): ZTF DR23 g = 15.03, r = 15.43,
  **Variable = False, Periodic = False**, Freq = 0, no class.
- Their search reached high frequencies (max detected 320 c/d; ours are 258.0 and 304.7 c/d), but every high-frequency
  pulsator they detected has a ZTF g amplitude of 0.07–0.11 mag (their V777 Her WDJ135309.97+484021.17: 489 s, 0.109;
  three ZZ Cetis at 270–305 s, 0.068–0.097). Our modes are ~0.013 in TESS, about 5–8x below the smallest of those,
  so a blind ZTF search is not expected to find them. Their non-detection is therefore consistent, not a refutation —
  but it means the variability has been looked at and not reported. Test: a ZTF fit at the TESS frequencies (below).
