# WDJ210219.83-123412.11 (GALEX J210219.8-123411, TIC 442532281) — candidate new ZZ Ceti

Found 2026-11-12 by `wdplanets/labs/zzceti.py` (backlog 28). Verdict: **candidate** (single sector).
**Updated 2026-11-13: known** — ZTF confirms the 454.6-s pulsation, but Huang et al. 2026 already published it (see the end).

- G = 16.54, d = 84.5 pc; Gaia XP class **DA** (P = 0.92, Jiménez-Esteban et al. 2023, VizieR J/MNRAS/518/5106),
  Teff 11,444 ± 162 K, log g 8.06 (same source); our catalogue TeffH 11,736 K, log g 8.10: inside the ZZ Ceti strip.
- TESS: only Sector 92 (20 s), CROWDSAP 0.35. Peak at **454.62 s** (2,199.6 µHz), 14.4 ppt = 6.3 <A>, 1.34x the
  amplitude of the 0.1 % false-alarm level from 1,000 shuffles (10.8 ppt). Present in both halves of the sector
  (12.5 ± 2.8 and 16.7 ± 2.5 ppt). No harmonic (2.7 ± 1.9 ppt). Romero et al. 2024 require 1.1x for one period: passes.
- Neighbours (Gaia DR3, 42"): one star at 26.4" (G 17.2, BP−RP 1.05), fainter than the WD.
- Literature: SIMBAD (Harvard mirror, 20" region with all object types): WD* only, no Pu*/ZZ*/V*; bibcodes listed on its page: 2018MNRAS.480.4505J, 2018yCat.1345....0G, 2020yCat.1350....0G, 2023MNRAS.518.5106J (catalogues and spectral classification). Not in the TIC tables of Romero et al. 2022 (S1-39) or 2024 (S40-69) - the star had no 120/20-s data before Sector 92, after their searches. Web search found no variability report.
- To confirm: a second TESS sector or ground-based photometry (≤ 30 s exposures, ~1 h).

## 2026-11-13 — ZTF targeted test and literature (verificador)

Verdict: **pulsating white dwarf confirmed by a second instrument (ZTF), but NOT new**: Huang et al. 2026
(ApJS 284, 72; arXiv:2605.03323, Appendix A table "White dwarf oscillators in this work", read in the arXiv HTML
version) list TIC 442532281, Sector 92, 190.043 c/d (= 2,199.6 µHz, 454.6 s), 14.758 ppt as one of their 24 new
pulsating white dwarfs. Our 2026-11-12 literature check missed it because we had read only their abstract.
Status → **known**.

- ZTF (`wdplanets/labs/ztf_target.py`, `data/labs/ztf_target/WDJ210219.83-123412.11/result.json`, figure
  `dashboard/img/ztf_target_WDJ210219.83-123412.11.png`): 441 g + 543 r epochs (30-s exposures, catflags 0),
  2018–2025. Window ±1 µHz around 2,199.636 µHz: coherent Δχ² (g+r) = 381 at **2,199.594 µHz** (0.04 µHz from TESS),
  2.9× the 0.1 % false-alarm threshold from 300 control windows; above all 300 controls; Gumbel p ≈ 1e-15.
  Amplitude 11.7 ppt (g), 12.3 ppt (r). Seasonal statistic also above all controls (2.2× threshold).
- Per season (8 per band, best frequency within ±0.5 µHz): best frequency at 2,199.57–2,199.61 µHz in 13 of 16
  band-seasons; amplitude (12–26 ppt) above the 95 % level of 40 control windows in 10 of 16 (single seasons have
  only 34–88 epochs).
- Resolved: ZTF sees only the white dwarf within 10" (Gaia's other source within 18" is G = 21.1); the 26" TESS
  neighbour is far outside ZTF's PSF, so the white dwarf is the pulsator.
- No ZTF systematic: four field stars 72–84" away show nothing at 2,199.6 or 3,814.7 µHz (0.6–0.7× threshold,
  p = 0.26–1; `field_check.csv`).
- **A second mode found blind in ZTF**: a full g+r ZTF spectrum 1,000–8,000 µHz has as its highest peaks 2,199.595 µHz,
  its 1-sidereal-day alias 2,187.99 µHz, and **3,814.72 µHz (262.14 s)** with its alias 3,826.33 µHz; nothing else
  comes close. In TESS Sector 92 the amplitude at 3,814.75 µHz is 9.70 ppt = 4.2 ⟨A⟩ (⟨A⟩ = 2.30 ppt), the highest
  peak in the 667–10,000 µHz band after the 454.6-s mode, but below our blind threshold (5.02 ⟨A⟩). The table row of
  Huang et al. we read lists only 190.043 c/d for this star, so the 262.14-s mode may not be published; a possible
  combination peak at 6,014.3 µHz (sum) has 7.0 ppt in TESS (3.1 ⟨A⟩, not significant).
- Injection test on the ZTF data: 50 % recovery at 6.7 ppt, 90 % at 10.7 ppt.
